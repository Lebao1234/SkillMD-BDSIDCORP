Powder coating or galvanizing: which coating for steel products

Both protect steel from rust, but each coating suits a different environment. A comparison of powder coating, hot-dip galvanizing and oil paint for racks, frames and gates.

In this article

  1. Powder coating
  2. Hot-dip galvanizing
  3. Oil paint
  4. Quick choice

Powder coating

Powder is sprayed on electrostatically and then cured at about 180 - 200 °C. The coat is even, comes in many colours and looks good, suited to furniture, racks, lockers, 5S tool boards and machine frames inside factories.

Hot-dip galvanizing

The part is dipped into a bath of molten zinc, so the zinc coats inner surfaces and hidden corners too. It gives the most durable rust protection for outdoor items: gates, fences, roof frames, fire escape stairs. The surface looks less refined than paint and can be painted over.

Oil paint

Low cost and easy to touch up, but the coat is thin and degrades quickly outdoors. Suited to temporary items or items that are repainted regularly.

Quick choice

  • Indoors, appearance matters: powder coating.
  • Outdoors, durability matters: hot-dip galvanizing, optionally painted over.
  • Low budget, temporary items: oil paint.
